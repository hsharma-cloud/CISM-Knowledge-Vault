> **Topic:** Governance vs. Management

## Purpose
Governance defines the direction, oversight, and accountability for information security, while management is responsible for planning, implementing, operating, and monitoring security activities. Understanding the distinction between governance and management is fundamental to CISM because security managers must align operational activities with the strategic direction established by leadership.
## Learning Objectives

> After completing this topic, the reader should be able to:
>
- Differentiate between governance and management.
- Explain the primary objectives of governance.
- Describe the responsibilities of management.
- Understand how governance and management work together to achieve business objectives.
## Overview
Governance and management are closely related but serve different purposes within an organization. Governance is the responsibility of the board of directors and executive leadership. It establishes strategic direction, defines risk tolerance, approves policies, and ensures that information security supports business objectives.

Management is responsible for executing that direction. Information security managers develop plans, implement security controls, manage projects, allocate resources, monitor performance, and report results back to leadership. Governance determines **what** should be achieved, while management determines **how** to achieve it.

## Key Terms
- **Governance:** Establishes strategic direction, oversight, and accountability to ensure information security supports business objectives.
- **Management:** Plans, implements, operates, and monitors security activities to achieve governance objectives.
- **Board of Directors:** Provides governance, approves strategy, and oversees organizational performance.
- **Executive Management:** Translates governance decisions into organizational direction and allocates resources.
## Detailed Explanation
Governance and management complement one another but have distinct responsibilities.

Governance focuses on strategic decision-making. It establishes the organization's vision for information security, defines risk appetite, approves policies, and ensures that security initiatives align with business objectives. Governance also provides oversight by reviewing performance and holding management accountable.

Management focuses on execution. Information security managers develop security programs, implement approved policies, manage personnel and budgets, monitor risks, measure performance, and report results to executive leadership. Their responsibility is to ensure that governance decisions are effectively translated into day-to-day operations.

Without governance, management lacks strategic direction. Without effective management, governance objectives cannot be successfully achieved. Both functions are essential for a mature information security program.
## Why It Matters
Effective governance ensures that information security investments support business goals, regulatory requirements, and the organization's risk appetite. Clear separation of governance and management responsibilities improves accountability, enables better decision-making, and helps organizations achieve consistent security outcomes.

## Manager's Perspective
An information security manager bridges the gap between governance and daily operations. The manager implements the strategic direction established by executive leadership, ensures security initiatives support business objectives, manages risks within the organization's risk appetite, measures performance, and regularly reports progress to leadership. Success is measured not only by technical controls but also by how effectively security enables the business.
## Real-World Example
A global financial institution's board approves an information security strategy that prioritizes protecting customer data and complying with financial regulations. Executive management allocates funding and establishes security objectives. The information security manager then implements security controls, oversees security operations, monitors key performance indicators, and reports progress to executive leadership. In this example, governance sets the direction, while management executes the strategy.
## Common Exam Tips
- Governance is performed by the board of directors and executive leadership.
- Management is responsible for planning, implementing, operating, and monitoring security activities.
- Governance answers **"What should be achieved?"**
- Management answers **"How will it be achieved?"**
- On the CISM exam, distinguish strategic responsibilities (governance) from operational responsibilities (management).
## Key Takeaways
- Governance establishes strategic direction and accountability.
- Management executes the strategy through planning, implementation, and operations.
- Governance focuses on business objectives and risk oversight.
- Management focuses on achieving governance objectives efficiently.
- Effective information security requires both governance and management working together.
